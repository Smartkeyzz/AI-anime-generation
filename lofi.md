# Building a Consistent Anime Lofi Wallpaper Set with PixAI Studio

Creating one good lofi wallpaper is easy enough. The harder part is creating several scenes that still feel like they belong to the same character, mood, and visual world.

For this project, I tested [PixAI Studio](https://eap.pixai.art/go/rhoda2)’s 2 A.M. workflow with my own African anime OC. Instead of manually creating every wallpaper from scratch, I used the existing workflow to expand one character and one visual direction into a complete set of matching scenes.

My goal was to keep the rainy late-night atmosphere of the original template while changing the color balance to better suit my character. I kept the blues, purples, and pinks associated with the workflow, but mixed in warmer amber lighting, brown undertones, mauve, and deeper purple tones.

The workflow generated eight scenes. From those, I picked three final wallpapers: a rainy window scene, a retro CRT bedroom, and a study scene featuring the OC.

Here’s how I created them.

## Start with the Character and Visual Direction

Before opening the workflow, I decided what kind of world I wanted the wallpapers to belong to.

My starting character was an African anime OC with warm brown skin, dark curly hair, and an oversized hoodie. I did not want her to appear in every wallpaper. Instead, I wanted the environments to look like places that naturally belonged to the same character.

![Image description](https://dev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/articles/6t3f1rddnu5ypkru9mv0.png)

The overall direction was a rainy late-night atmosphere with warm indoor lighting, muted blue and purple night tones, soft pink and mauve accents, cozy bedroom and city environments, and a slightly melancholic lofi mood.

That direction became the visual anchor for the entire set.

The biggest change I made was to the palette. The original **2 A.M., Rain** template leaned toward cooler pastel colors that worked well with its silver-haired character. For my version, I added warmer browns, amber light, dusty pink, and deeper purple tones.

The idea was not to completely replace the original aesthetic. I wanted to keep the recognizable lofi look while making the world feel more connected to my OC. 

## Clone the 2 A.M., Rain Workflow

I started with the existing **2 A.M., [Rain — Lofi Live Wallpaper** template](https://blog.pixai.art/en/how-to-use-pixai-studio-from-character-photoshoots-to-ai-animation-10-templates-you-can-clone-instantly/#:~:text=Lofi%20Live%20Wallpaper%20%7C%20Turn%20a%20Still%20Frame%20Into%20a%20Quiet%20Moment%20of%20Company) rather than building a [workflow](https://blog.pixai.art/en/how-to-use-pixai-studio-from-character-photoshoots-to-ai-animation-10-templates-you-can-clone-instantly/) manually.

After opening the template, I selected **Clone workspace** to create an editable version inside PixAI Studio.

![Image description](https://dev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/articles/oofbbhij9ifm3p0hfsrm.jpg)

The workflow already had the scene structure I needed, so there was no reason to rebuild it.

I replaced the original character input with my own OC and then adjusted the visual direction of the generated scenes.

The original palette relied heavily on pink, lavender, turquoise, and blue. I kept those colors but introduced warmer amber lighting, cocoa-brown undertones, dusty rose, and darker purple accents.

The process was basically:

**Clone workspace → Replace the starting image → Adjust the visual direction → Generate the scene set**

That was enough to customize the existing template without turning the project into a node-by-node rebuild. 

## Generate the Eight Lofi Scenes

Once the starting character and visual direction were ready, I ran the workflow.

It produced a full set of eight lofi scenes.

![Image description](https://dev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/articles/hpnttxvzp3ytnt62ath4.jpg)

I deliberately avoided treating each scene as a completely separate concept. The goal was for the environments to change while the overall world remained recognizable.

That worked fairly well.

Most of the images kept the same warm nighttime atmosphere, combining amber indoor lighting with muted blues, purples, and pinks.

The environments were much more varied. The set included rainy windows, a retro bedroom, city views, small desk objects, and character-focused scenes.

The OC also remained recognizable in the images where she appeared. More importantly, the environment-only scenes still felt connected to her because they shared the same lighting and color direction.

Three results stood out almost immediately: the rainy window, the CRT bedroom, and the study scene.

I stopped at the image stage and did not continue into the Video Nodes because I only needed still wallpapers for this test. 

## Choosing the Three Best Lofi Wallpapers

After generating all eight scenes, I compared them based on composition, background space, lighting, character placement, and how well they worked as actual wallpapers rather than simply attractive illustrations. 

### Wallpaper 1: Rainy Window

![Image description](https://dev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/articles/zd4z6r2eberxmy863ghj.png)

The rainy window had the cleanest composition.

The large window gave the scene plenty of open space, while the rain streaks and blurred city lights added atmosphere without making the image feel crowded.

It also worked naturally as a desktop background because there was enough breathing room around the main visual elements.

The warm amber light around the window balanced nicely against the muted purple and blue tones outside. 

### Wallpaper 2: Retro CRT Bedroom

![Image description](https://dev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/articles/5tglu8h5105fmexx4iqi.png)

The CRT bedroom had much more detail, but it still worked well as a wallpaper.

The stacked monitors, books, reflective wooden floor, and city view made the room feel lived-in without completely overwhelming the composition.

I especially liked the way the warmer browns and amber lighting interacted with the blue and purple glow from the screens and window.

It felt more visually complex than the rainy window scene while still belonging to the same late-night world. 

### Wallpaper 3: Study Scene

![Image description](https://dev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/articles/mgwatgzk9cdl2rlsx216.png)

The study scene was the most personal result because it brought the OC directly into the environment.

The desk lamp created a strong warm light around the character while the cooler city lights remained visible in the background.

The relaxed pose, notebook, and desk setup also fit naturally with the quiet late-night lofi mood.

This image was less minimal than the other two, but it gave the final selection something the environment-only wallpapers could not: a clear connection back to the character that started the workflow. 

## Which Wallpaper Worked Best?

![Image description](https://dev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/articles/r6jtuend4byvs9khab2m.png)

Seeing the three results side by side made their strengths much easier to compare.

The **rainy window** had the most open space and the simplest composition. It would probably be the easiest to use as a desktop wallpaper because icons would not have to compete with many foreground details.

The **CRT bedroom** was more detailed and had the strongest environmental personality. The retro monitors and reflected lighting gave it a very clear lofi-room identity.

The **study scene** had the strongest character presence. The OC remained easy to recognize, while the warm desk light helped her fit naturally into the same visual world as the other scenes.

My favorite was the **CRT bedroom**.

It had the best balance of atmosphere, detail, usable background space, and the warm-versus-cool color contrast I was trying to achieve throughout the project. 

## What I Learned About Making Lofi Wallpapers

The most detailed image is not automatically the best wallpaper.

The results that worked best had enough space around the main subject, soft lighting, a consistent palette, and a composition that did not feel overloaded.

The rainy window worked because it had space to breathe. The CRT bedroom had much more detail, but the objects were arranged clearly enough that they did not compete with one another. In the study scene, the character was prominent without completely hiding the environment.

The color palette also mattered more than I expected. Repeating the same warm amber, blue, purple, pink, and brown tones across different environments helped make otherwise very different images feel like part of the same set.

A good lofi wallpaper needs atmosphere, but it also needs restraint. If every part of the image demands attention, it starts feeling more like an illustration than something you would comfortably use as a background. 

## Reuse the Workflow with a Different Lofi Theme

The same cloned workflow does not have to stay tied to this exact rainy-night direction.

You could reuse it with another character and switch the overall mood to something like a snowy study room, late-night café, train-window scene, quiet library, neon apartment, or sunset bedroom.

The important part is keeping one clear visual direction for each run.

Instead of manually designing several unrelated scenes, give the workflow a consistent character, palette, and atmosphere, then let it expand that idea across the different environments. 

## Final Thoughts

The main advantage of using the **2 A.M., Rain** workflow was not simply that it generated several images.

It was that I could start with one character and visual direction, reuse an existing [Studio](https://blog.pixai.art/en/pixai-studio-the-ultimate-all-in-one-anime-creation-workspace/) setup, and turn that idea into a cohesive wallpaper set without manually constructing eight separate scenes.

For this test, the process was straightforward: clone the workflow, replace the starting image, adjust the mood and palette, generate eight scenes, then select the results that work best as wallpapers.

If you already have an OC, trying the same workflow with a visual direction built around that character is an easy way to create a lofi wallpaper set that feels more personal than using the original template unchanged.
