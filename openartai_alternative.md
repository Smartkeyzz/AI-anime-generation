### How to Start Creating Anime Art With PixAI
Getting started with AI-generated anime art doesn't require mastering every feature on day one. A better approach is to build a simple workflow, understand what each tool does, and gradually refine your process as you gain experience.

Here's a beginner-friendly workflow that works well on PixAI.

### Step 1: Choose an Anime-Focused Model
Your model has the biggest influence on the final image. Different anime models are trained on different styles, so the same prompt can produce very different results depending on the model you choose.
If you're just starting out, experiment with a few popular anime models before worrying about advanced settings. Finding a model whose style matches your goals will save time later.
### Step 2: Write a Clear Character Prompt
Start with a simple description of the character instead of trying to include every possible detail.
For example:
1girl, original anime character, long silver hair, blue eyes,
black hoodie, standing on a rooftop at sunset,
confident smile, detailed anime illustration,
vibrant lighting, high quality

Once you're comfortable with the basics, you can gradually add details such as camera angles, lighting, clothing materials, facial expressions, or environmental effects.

### Step 3: Learn From Community Models
One advantage of platforms like PixAI is the active community. Browse public models, prompts, and example images to understand how experienced creators achieve different visual styles.
Instead of copying prompts exactly, study how they're structured and identify which changes have the biggest impact on the final image.

### Step 4: Add LoRAs When You Need More Control
After you've generated a few successful images, start experimenting with LoRAs.
LoRAs let you introduce specific styles, clothing designs, facial features, poses, or character traits without making your prompts unnecessarily long. They become especially useful when you're trying to create recurring characters or maintain a consistent artistic style.

### Step 5: Refine Instead of Starting Over
One mistake beginners often make is regenerating an entire image because one detail isn't quite right.
A more efficient approach is to use reference images and editing tools to improve only the areas that need adjustment. Small refinements to expressions, hairstyles, accessories, or composition can produce better results than repeatedly generating completely new images.

### Step 6: Build Your Own Workflow
As you create more artwork, save the prompts, models, LoRAs, and reference images that consistently produce results you like.
Over time, you'll develop a personal workflow that makes future projects faster and more consistent. Instead of beginning every illustration from scratch, you'll have a reusable foundation that you can adapt for new characters and ideas.
