# Beyond Costume Changes: How to Build Alternate-Universe Characters with AI

Spider-Man is one of those characters you can recognize almost instantly. But what happens when you keep the character recognizable while completely changing the universe he belongs to?

In this experiment, I used [PixAI Studio](https://eap.pixai.art/go/rhoda2) with [Tsubaki.3](https://blog.pixai.art/en/tsubaki-3-alpha-showcase/) to explore three different interpretations of Spider-Man. Instead of simply creating new costumes, I wanted to see what would happen if the environment, materials, lighting, atmosphere, and overall visual style were transformed as well.

The result was three distinct alternate universes: Cyberpunk Spider-Man, set in a neon-filled futuristic city; Samurai Spider-Man, redesigned for a historical Japanese-inspired world; and Dark Fantasy Spider-Man, placed in a gothic setting filled with mist, ancient architecture, and a massive red moon.

The goal throughout was simple: change the world without losing the character.

## Build a Simple Alternate-Universe Workflow

I started by opening [PixAI Studio](https://eap.pixai.art/go/rhoda2) and creating a new workspace for the experiment. Keeping everything in one workspace made it easier to organize the different creative directions and compare the final results side by side.

For the image generation, I used Tsubaki.3 consistently throughout the workflow. Using the same model for each version helped keep the generation setup consistent, allowing me to focus more on how the creative direction changed from one universe to another.

I began with a basic Spider-Man concept and then created separate Image Nodes for the three alternate universes: Cyberpunk, Samurai, and Dark Fantasy.Each branch had a specific purpose. Rather than changing only Spider-Man's costume, I also changed the world setting, materials, environment, lighting, and atmosphere around him. This approach made each version feel like it belonged to a completely different universe while keeping the overall workflow straightforward.

## Keep the Core Character, Change the World

Before creating the three alternate universes, I started with a simple Spider-Man concept. The purpose of this base generation was to establish the recognizable features I wanted to carry through all three versions.

Base Prompt:

Spider-Man, recognizable red and blue color palette, black web-pattern details, large white eye lenses, spider emblem on chest, athletic build, full-body character, highly detailed, dramatic cinematic composition.

For images, refer to [substack](https://rhodasmart.substack.com/p/create-3-alternate-universe-spider?r=2cbcht&utm_campaign=post-expanded-share&utm_medium=web)
Starting from this foundation gave me a consistent character to work with. From there, I could change much more than just the costume. Each universe introduced different materials, environments, props, lighting, and visual moods, while the core Spider-Man identity remained intact.

## Universe 1: Cyberpunk Spider-Man

The first alternate universe takes Spider-Man into a futuristic megacity filled with neon lights, towering skyscrapers, and advanced technology.

For this version, I wanted the environment to feel dramatically different while keeping enough of Spider-Man’s original design for the character to remain immediately recognizable.

Cyberpunk Prompt:

Reimagine Spider-Man in a futuristic cyberpunk universe. Give his red and blue suit advanced high-tech materials with subtle glowing web patterns and illuminated details while keeping his white eye lenses and spider emblem recognizable. Place him in a massive neon megacity at night, surrounded by futuristic skyscrapers, holographic signs, glowing advertisements, flying vehicles, and rain-soaked streets reflecting neon lights. Dark futuristic atmosphere, blue and magenta city glow, cinematic lighting, detailed sci-fi world, dramatic perspective.

The generated result kept Spider-Man’s familiar red-and-blue suit, white eye lenses, web patterns, and chest emblem, but completely transformed the world around him. Futuristic buildings, digital billboards, neon lighting, and a flying vehicle make the setting feel far more technologically advanced.

The blue and magenta lighting creates a strong contrast with the red suit, while reflections on the rooftop and surrounding buildings strengthen the rainy cyberpunk atmosphere.

What I liked most about this version is that it feels futuristic without turning Spider-Man into a completely robotic character. His suit gets a high-tech upgrade, but his core appearance remains easy to recognize.

## Universe 2: Samurai Spider-Man

For the second universe, I moved Spider-Man away from the futuristic city and into a historical Japanese-inspired setting. This time, I wanted the character design itself to change more dramatically while still preserving the details that make him recognizable as Spider-Man.

Samurai Prompt:

Reimagine Spider-Man in a historical Japanese-inspired samurai universe. Give him layered red and dark blue samurai-inspired armor with Spider-Man web patterns worked into the armor, recognizable white eye lenses, spider emblem details, armored forearms and traditional warrior elements. Place him on the rooftops of a feudal Japanese city at night, surrounded by traditional wooden buildings, tiled roofs, lanterns, distant mountains, and drifting mist. Katana-inspired visual elements, dramatic moonlight, cinematic atmosphere, detailed historical environment.

The Samurai version produced the biggest character redesign so far. Instead of a conventional superhero suit, Spider-Man now appears in layered armor with large shoulder pieces, armored forearms, traditional footwear, and a sword. Even with these changes, the red-and-dark color scheme, white eye lenses, web patterns, and spider emblem keep him recognizable.

The environment also does a lot of the storytelling. Traditional rooftops, wooden buildings, lanterns, distant mountains, and moonlight replace the futuristic skyscrapers from the Cyberpunk version.

What stood out to me most was how much the armor changed Spider-Man’s overall silhouette. While the Cyberpunk version stayed relatively close to his familiar appearance, the Samurai version feels like a character genuinely redesigned to belong in another historical world.

## Universe 3: Dark Fantasy Spider-Man

For the final universe, I moved Spider-Man away from both science fiction and historical inspiration and placed him in a much darker, supernatural setting.

This version combines gothic architecture, fantasy elements, dramatic lighting, and an ominous atmosphere to create a world that feels completely different from the first two designs.

Dark Fantasy Prompt:

Reimagine Spider-Man in a dark fantasy universe. Give him a dark red and black Spider-Man suit combined with subtle ancient fantasy armor, recognizable white eye lenses, web-pattern details and spider emblem, with mysterious glowing web-like magical energy around his hands. Place him above an ancient gothic city filled with towering stone architecture, distant castle spires, thick mist, and a giant moon dominating the night sky. Dark medieval atmosphere, dramatic shadows, eerie red moonlight, cinematic fantasy lighting, mysterious heroic mood, highly detailed.

The result immediately feels different from the other two universes. A massive red moon dominates the background, while gothic towers, thick mist, and the dark city below create a much more mysterious and ominous atmosphere.

Spider-Man’s traditional red-and-blue palette is replaced with dark red and black, giving the character a more supernatural appearance. However, his white eye lenses, spider emblem, and web-pattern details remain, helping preserve his recognizable identity.

The glowing, web-like red energy around his hand adds another layer of fantasy to the design without taking attention away from the character.

What Makes the Dark Fantasy Version Work?

I especially liked how the environment contributes to the overall design. Rather than filling the scene with too many fantasy elements, the giant moon, gothic architecture, mist, and red lighting are enough to establish the dark fantasy universe while keeping Spider-Man as the main focus.

With all three versions complete, the differences become much easier to appreciate when the Cyberpunk, Samurai, and Dark Fantasy designs are viewed together. Each one transforms Spider-Man in a different way while maintaining recognizable elements of the original character.

## Compare the Three Alternate Universes

Putting the three versions side by side makes it easier to see how much a single character can change when you alter the costume materials, world setting, lighting, and atmosphere.

The Cyberpunk Spider-Man stays closest to the familiar design. His red-and-blue suit, white lenses, web patterns, and overall silhouette remain recognizable, while the neon megacity, futuristic architecture, and blue-magenta lighting establish the new universe.

The Samurai Spider-Man changes the character design the most. Layered armor, large shoulder pieces, traditional footwear, and the sword significantly alter his silhouette. However, the red coloring, white lenses, web details, and spider emblem keep him from becoming just a generic samurai character.

The Dark Fantasy Spider-Man creates the biggest shift in atmosphere. Its dark red-and-black palette, gothic architecture, thick mist, glowing web-like energy, and enormous red moon make the world feel supernatural and threatening.

Of the three, I found Samurai Spider-Man to be the biggest overall transformation because both the clothing materials and silhouette changed considerably. Cyberpunk Spider-Man remained the most recognizable, since much of his familiar design stayed intact while the environment carried most of the alternate-universe transformation.

Together, these results show that creating an alternate-universe character doesn't have to mean simply changing the outfit. Changing the world around the character can be just as important as redesigning the character itself.
Try Your Own Spider-Man Universe

Once you have a recognizable core character, you can use the same approach to explore countless alternate universes. The key is to think beyond the costume and consider how the entire world would change around the character.

You could experiment with ideas such as a Western or cowboy universe, a large-scale space opera, a post-apocalyptic city, a mechanical steampunk world, a magical academy, or even a retro-futuristic New York.

You don't have to generate every idea at once. Start with one universe and identify what visually defines it. Think about the materials Spider-Man would wear, the architecture around him, the props that belong in that world, and the lighting and atmosphere that would make the setting believable.

The main principle remains simple: keep enough recognizable Spider-Man elements to preserve the character's identity while giving yourself freedom to transform the world around him.

## My Favorite Spider-Man Universe on PixAI

Of the three alternate-universe designs, Dark Fantasy Spider-Man was my favorite. The giant red moon, gothic architecture, mist, and dark red-and-black color palette created the strongest atmosphere of the three concepts.

What stood out most was how different the world felt without losing Spider-Man’s identity. His white eye lenses, spider emblem, and web-pattern details remained recognizable, while the glowing web-like energy introduced a supernatural element that fit naturally into the fantasy setting.

I also liked how the environment supported the character instead of overwhelming him. The gothic towers and enormous red moon establish the dark fantasy setting immediately, while Spider-Man remains the clear focus of the composition.

## Conclusion

Creating these alternate-universe versions of Spider-Man showed just how much a character can change when you redesign the world, not just the costume.

Using Tsubaki.3 in [PixAI Studio](https://blog.pixai.art/en/pixai-studio-the-ultimate-all-in-one-anime-creation-workspace/), I explored three distinct directions: a neon-filled Cyberpunk future, a historical Samurai world, and a supernatural Dark Fantasy universe. Each version introduced different materials, environments, lighting, and atmospheres while preserving enough familiar details to keep Spider-Man recognizable.

The same workflow can be applied to your own original characters. Start with a clear character identity, create different universe concepts, and experiment with how far you can transform the surrounding world without losing the features that make the character recognizable.

You can also create your own scenes and participate in the PixAI [Community Contest](https://pixai.art/en/contest) to share your results.

## Final Thoughts

This experiment showed me that AI character design can go far beyond simply creating a new costume. By changing the world, materials, lighting, environment, and atmosphere, the same Spider-Man concept can become something that feels completely new while still retaining its identity.

The three versions each brought something different to the table. Cyberpunk Spider-Man felt futuristic and technologically advanced, Samurai Spider-Man delivered the biggest character redesign, and Dark Fantasy Spider-Man created the strongest overall atmosphere.

For me, the biggest takeaway is that the setting can be just as important as the character. If you start with a recognizable foundation and then carefully build a new world around it, AI image generation gives you plenty of room to experiment with creative possibilities.

So, instead of asking “What would this character wear?”, try asking “What kind of world would this character belong to?” That small change in approach can lead to some surprisingly different results.
